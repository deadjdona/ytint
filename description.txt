A YouTube comment corpus is unusually rich because each comment carries text + author identity + timestamp + engagement metrics + reply-tree structure + linkage back to a specific video (and its metadata). That lets you cross several axes simultaneously. Below is a maximized list, grouped by analysis family, with the visualisations each one suggests.

0. Data dimensions you can leverage
Before the list — what's actually in the data:

Content: comment text, parent/child (thread structure), quoted timestamp mentions like "@2:30"
Author: channel_id, display name, recurrence across videos, account age (sometimes)
Time: published_at, updated_at, reply latency
Engagement: like_count, reply_count, hearted-by-creator, pinned
Video context: video_id, title, tags, category, duration, publish time, view/like counts
Derived: language, sentiment, embeddings, topics, toxicity, bot-likelihood
1. Temporal / attention dynamics
Comment velocity curve per video (comments/hour) → half-life of discussion
Decay / survival curve: probability a comment thread is still active at t
Diurnal + weekly heatmaps (hour × day-of-week) of posting volume
Upload-event alignment: comments-per-minute in the first N hours after publish
Second-derivative spikes: detecting "revival" events (a video re-trending)
Thread reply latency distribution (how fast do replies come?)
Cohort retention: commenters who arrived at video N — do they return at video N+1?
Comment "shelf life": time-window where 90% of likes accumulate
Visualisations: streamgraph of volume over time, horizon charts for many videos, calendar heatmaps, Kaplan–Meier-style thread survival curves, ridge plots of velocity per video.

2. Topic & semantic
Topic modelling (LDA / BERTopic / Top2Vec) over the whole corpus
Topic × video matrix — which videos discuss what
Topic prevalence over time (within a video's comment lifetime and across uploads)
Semantic embeddings (USE / SBERT / OpenAI) → UMAP/t-SNE scatter coloured by video, topic, sentiment
Semantic drift: how the meaning of a term shifts across videos or over months
Keyword / n-gram frequency with TF-IDF weighting per video
Co-occurrence networks of terms or hashtags
Named entity / product / person mentions extraction
Question vs statement vs command classification
Intent taxonomy: praise, complaint, question, suggestion, off-topic, spam
Visualisations: UMAP cluster scatter, streamgraph of topic share, treemap of topic hierarchy, alluvial diagram of topic→video, word co-occurrence graph, keyword-in-context concordances.

3. Sentiment & affect
Valence distribution per video / per topic
Sentiment trajectory over a video's lifetime or within threads
Emotion classification (joy, anger, fear, surprise, sadness, disgust) — Plutchik wheel
Sarcasm / irony detection (hard, but tractable with tuned models)
Toxicity / hate / profanity prevalence, and which videos/topics attract it
Polarity vs engagement — do negative comments get more likes/replies?
Creator-positive vs creator-negative ratio per video
Sentiment divergence between top-level and nested replies (do replies amplify or counter?)
Visualisations: emotion wheel, sentiment ridge plots, valence-arousal scatter, diverging bar charts per topic, sentiment-over-time line charts with upload events marked.

4. Linguistic & stylistic
Language distribution across the corpus
Lexical richness (type-token ratio, Yule's K, MTLD) by video/author
Readability (Flesch etc.) distribution
Emoji usage frequency, diversity, and per-topic emoji signatures
Emoji-sentiment mapping
Slang / internet-register lexicon frequency
Comment length distributions (chars, tokens), and length vs likes
ALL-CAPS / repetition / punctuation intensity (often a proxy for emotional intensity)
Code-switching detection (multilingual comments)
Hashtag / @mention extraction and networks
Visualisations: emoji frequency treemaps, length-vs-likes hexbin, language stack bars, readability violin plots.

5. Network / graph — the structural goldmine
Reply-tree forest: directed graph of comment → reply, per video and across corpus
Author–video bipartite graph: who commented where
Author co-commenting graph (projection: authors linked if they comment on the same videos) — this reveals cliques/tribes
"Who replies to whom" author network (stronger signal than co-commenting)
Community detection (Louvain, Leiden, Infomap) on these graphs
Centrality analysis: who gets replied to most (in-degree), who bridges communities (betweenness)
K-core decomposition of the reply graph
Bow-tie structure (strongly connected core, in/out tendrils)
Clique enumeration (literal cliques of mutually-interacting commenters)
Reciprocity of reply relationships
Author specialisation: commenters who only appear in one video's subtree
Visualisations: force-directed network layouts (with communities coloured), hive plots, arc diagrams of reply flows, bipartite two-mode layouts, sankey of commenter flow between video-communities.

6. Cohorts & segmentation
RFM segmentation of commenters — Recency (last comment), Frequency (comments per month), Monetary (likes received)
Behavioural cohorts: lurkers (1 comment), regulars (recurring), power users, super-engagers
Acquisition cohort by upload event (commenters who first appeared at video N)
Cross-video overlap matrix: Jaccard of author sets between each pair of videos
Author "fan loyalty": fraction of an author's comments going to one channel
New vs returning commenter share per video
Topic-cohort: people who talk about topic X — do they cluster on certain videos?
Visualisations: cohort retention curves, RFM scatter (3D or 2D slices), sankey of cohort migration, Jaccard heatmap (video × video), churn/retention matrices.

7. Engagement & attention economy
Like-count distribution (almost always a power law / heavy tail)
Gini coefficient of attention inequality per video
Top-K comment concentration (do 1% of comments get 80% of likes?)
Reply depth distribution (how deep do threads go?)
Branching factor of threads
"Pinned/hearted" effect on downstream engagement
First-mover advantage: do early comments get more likes?
Position bias: does comment order (sort) drive engagement?
Attention transfer: does a popular comment pull likes to its parent/siblings?
Visualisations: log-log rank/frequency plots, Lorenz curves, Pareto frontiers, depth-vs-likes scatter, branching-factor histograms.

8. Thread / conversational structure
Thread depth & width distributions
Conversation resolution patterns (question answered? argument unresolved?)
Thread sentiment trajectory (does it escalate / de-escalate?)
Reply chain length distribution
Who-initiates-who-responds patterns
Topic evolution within a thread (does the subject drift?)
Visualisations: sunburst of thread trees, dendrograms, thread-trajectory ridge plots, sankey of topic transitions within threads.

9. Comparative & cross-video
Video profile radar (sentiment, toxicity, depth, likes, topic entropy)
Channel-to-channel comparison (if corpus spans multiple channels)
Before/after analysis around a creator pivot, controversy, or format change
Series vs standalone video comparison
Category benchmarking (gaming vs education vs music comments behave very differently)
Visualisations: parallel coordinates, radar charts, small multiples, dumbbell plots.

10. Anomaly, spam & integrity
Bot / astroturf detection (temporal bursts, repeated phrasing, near-duplicate clusters, account age skew)
Near-duplicate / copy-paste comment clustering (MinHash)
Coordinated commenting / brigading (sudden influx of new authors with aligned sentiment on one video)
Spam template discovery
Topic injection: external topics suddenly appearing in a video's comments
Sentiment anomalies: unusual negativity spikes
Suspicious like-inflation (like-count distribution breaks from the power-law norm)
Visualisations: anomaly-scatter on the timeline, duplicate-cluster dendrograms, "new authors per hour" overlay charts, anomaly heatmap.

11. Author-level / identity
Power-law of commenter activity (1% of authors = X% of comments)
Author persistence: return rate across uploads
Author "fingerprint": their typical topics, sentiment, length
Cross-channel authors (audience overlap with other channels if data allows)
Display-name reuse and impersonation risk
Visualisations: rank-frequency (Zipf) plots, author "fingerprint" heatmaps, returning-vs-new stacked bars.

12. Cross-modal — comments ↔ video content
Timestamp-mention extraction ("@3:45", "at 2:30") → map reactions onto the video timeline
Moment-level reaction heatmap along the video's duration
Comment–transcript alignment: do comments cluster around certain scenes/words?
Topic match between comment and video tags — are viewers on-topic or off-topic?
Spoiler / plot-reference detection
Reaction type by scene (laughing moments, confusion spikes)
Visualisations: video-timeline ribbon with comment density peaks, aligned transcript+comment dual heatmap, peak-annotation timeline.

13. Predictive & causal
Predict comment likes from features (length, sentiment, position, author karma, topic, hour-of-day)
Predict video engagement from early-comment signals (first-1-hour comment velocity → final count)
Feature importance / SHAP on those models
Predict toxicity likelihood from thread context
Early-burst detection: which comment will go viral?
Propensity models: likelihood a commenter returns
Visualisations: SHAP summary plots, lift curves, confusion matrices, feature-importance bars, ROC/PR curves.

14. Meta & corpus-quality
Comment-volume vs view-count scaling (does the video over- or under-perform?)
Engagement rate (comments per 1k views)
Missing/comment-disabled video flagging
Sampling-bias audit (YouTube only returns a sample — quantify it)
Language coverage gaps
Visualisations: log-log scatter of views vs comments, funnel charts, coverage matrices.

A few cross-cutting combinations that are especially interesting
Topic × sentiment × time (3-way streamgraph) — see how a topic's mood shifts
Cohort × community — do "early joiners" and "latecomers" form different tribes?
Network centrality × toxicity — are the most-replied-to people the most toxic, or the most helpful?
Reply-depth × sentiment trajectory — do deep threads polarise?
Timestamped reactions × transcript — a true "audience reaction map" of the video itself
